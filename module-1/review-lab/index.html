Remember

Hydrolysis is the reverse of condensation: water adds across the compound group and gives back the simple groups. Hydrolysis of any anhydride is highly exergonic, which is why anhydrides store chemical energy. Glutamic acid and glutamate are the same molecule in different protonation states.

Free energy

From: Review of Thermodynamics for Biochem, Parts 1 and 2

Predict first: when is a process spontaneous?

Use ΔG = ΔH − TΔS. For each sign combination, when is ΔG negative?

Test it: ΔG = ΔH − TΔS

Load a scenario from the videos or set your own values. T must be in kelvin: K = °C + 273.15. ΔS is usually given in J/(mol·K), so divide by 1000 to match kJ.

ΔG depends on concentration

ΔG = ΔG°′ + RT ln Q and ΔG°′ = −RT ln K′eq. The prime means the biochemical standard state: 1 M reactants and products, 1 atm, 25 °C, and pH 7. R = 8.3145 J/(mol·K), T = 298.15 K.

Water

From: Water, Parts 1 and 2

Rank the bonding interactions, strongest first

You don't need to memorize the values, except that an H bond is about 20 kJ/mol. You do need the order.

Correct H bond or not?

An H bond needs an H on an electronegative donor (N, O, F, Cl) and an electronegative acceptor. Never draw one to the H of a C–H.

Osmosis: what happens to the cell?

The solute can't cross the membrane, so water moves toward the side with more solute. Inside the cell is fixed at 300 mOsm/L.

Your blood buffer

CO₂ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. Using the rounded pKa of 6.4: pH = 6.4 + log([HCO₃⁻]/[CO₂]). Your lungs control CO₂ and your kidneys control HCO₃⁻.
